# Vibe City: The Seaport and Ships
Audience: player
Keywords: seaport, port, harbour, harbor, dock, quay, ships, freighter, tanker, container ship, shipping, sea, coast, shore, industry, industrial demand, trade, jobs, placement, open sea, Industry needs a seaport
Summary: The Seaport is a $6,000 two by two dock that goes on the shore in Vibe City. With power, water and a way out to open sea, ships call at it, it lifts industrial demand across the city and gives 20 jobs. A coastal city past 2,500 people needs one, or its industry is held back.

## What the Seaport is
The Seaport is Vibe City's version of the SimCity 2000 seaport. You find it in the Transport drawer of the build menu, after Road, Tunnel, Rail and Rail Depot. It is a 2 by 2 building: a dock, a crane and a quay. It costs $6,000 to build (1.6 times that on high hill ground) and $40 a month upkeep. It uses 4 MW of power and 3 units of water per tile. Its job is trade: it ships your city's goods out by sea, which helps industry, and it is a workplace with 20 jobs when open.

## Where you can build a Seaport
In Vibe City a Seaport has stricter placement rules than most buildings. The 2 by 2 square must be:
- entirely clear ground (grass, or trees, which come down for it),
- not under water anywhere, and with no tunnel bore beneath it,
- all at one height (level ground),
- and on the shore, with water along at least one side of the square.
The cursor tells you why a spot will not work before you click: "A seaport goes on the shore, with water along one side", "The ground under it has to be level", "The whole square has to be clear ground" or "Part of that square is under water".

The quay faces whichever side has the most water along it, with water that reaches the sea counting extra, and the building's picture turns so its quay is on that side.

## When a Seaport works
A Seaport in Vibe City only works when all three of these are true:
1. It has power.
2. It has water.
3. The water beside it reaches the open sea. Open sea means water joined to the edge of the map.
A dock on a landlocked lake or a pond you dug yourself never works: no ship has anywhere to come from. When you build one, a toast warns you if something is missing: "Seaport built, but this water never reaches the open sea, so no ship can call", or "Seaport built. Ships will call once it has power and water". The tile inspector's Harbour row shows the status: "Open, ships calling", "No way out to open sea", "No water beside it", or the reason it is closed.

## What a working Seaport does for the city
A working Seaport in Vibe City does two things for the economy:
- It lifts industrial demand across the whole city by 0.15. A second working port adds half as much again (0.075 more), and past two there is no more trade to be had.
- It gives 20 jobs while open.
The tile inspector sums this up as "Trade: Lifts industry across the city". A shut port gives neither. Like any civic building it also draws on your power and water, and its $40 a month shows in City Stats with the other civic upkeep. Raised industrial demand means more factories want to build and grow, which brings jobs for your residents.

## Why a big coastal city needs one
In Vibe City a city by the sea that grows to 2,500 people or more finds its industry stalls without a seaport. Until a port is working, industrial demand is held to at most 0.2. This only applies when the map has open sea at its edge; a landlocked city is never asked for a seaport.

You will know when it bites. The Demand window (click the demand meter) lists "Industry needs a seaport. A city this size by the sea ships its goods out by boat. Build one on the shore with power and water." and the archivist says "The factories have nowhere to ship to, Mayor. A seaport on the coast would open things up, nya." Build a port on the coast, give it power and water, and the cap lifts at once.

## Ships
Ships come and go from every working Seaport in Vibe City. Each working port has at most one ship at a time, and there are never more than 8 ships on the map. A new ship is sighted 6 to 22 seconds after the last one left. It sails in from a random point on the edge of the map, fading into view, follows the open water to the quay, slows as it comes in, swings alongside and stays tied up for 10 seconds. Then it sails back out the way it came and fades away off the edge. Ships move at about 1.3 tiles a second and leave a wake behind them.

There are three kinds of ship: two container freighters (one carries its own deck cranes) and a tanker. If a port is shut down or bulldozed, its ship turns round and leaves. Ships are scenery: they show your port is trading, and you do not control them.

## Tips for the Seaport
- Check the water first. The quick test is whether that water connects to the map's edge. A river or bay that does is fine; a lake that does not is useless.
- Build near the coast before you reach 2,500 people, so industry never stalls.
- Run power lines and water pipes to it like any civic building, or it stays closed.
- A second port is worth half as much again; a third adds nothing more.
- Industry near the port is not required: the boost applies to industrial demand citywide.

## Common questions
**Q: How much does a Seaport cost?**
A: $6,000 to build and $40 a month. It needs power and water.

**Q: How big is a Seaport?**
A: 2 by 2 tiles, on clear, level ground with water along at least one side.

**Q: Why will my Seaport not work?**
A: It needs power, water, and water beside it that is joined to the edge of the map. A landlocked lake will not do.

**Q: When does my city need a Seaport?**
A: When the map has open sea and the city reaches 2,500 people. Without a working port, industrial demand is held to 0.2.

**Q: Does a landlocked city need a Seaport?**
A: No. A map with no sea at its edge is never asked for one.

**Q: How much does a Seaport help industry?**
A: A working port adds 0.15 to industrial demand citywide; a second adds 0.075 more; more than two add nothing.

**Q: How many jobs does a Seaport give?**
A: 20 when open.

**Q: How many ships can there be?**
A: One per working port at a time, and at most 8 on the map.

**Q: Can I build a Seaport on a pond I dug?**
A: You can build it if the shore rules are met, but it will never work unless that water connects to the map's edge.
