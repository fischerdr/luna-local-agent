# Vibe City: Power Plants, Power Lines and the Grid
Audience: player
Keywords: power, electricity, power plant, coal plant, solar farm, wind farm, generator, power line, poles, wires, grid, MW, megawatts, blackout, brownout, no power, power shortage, coverage, upkeep, utilities, hill bonus
Summary: In Vibe City power comes from three kinds of generator: the power plant, the solar farm and the wind farm. Power lines carry it to the city, and buildings that have power pass it on to the buildings touching them. Without power, lots barely grow, built lots empty out, and civic buildings and water pumps shut down.

## The three generators
Vibe City has three generators, all in the Utilities group of the build menu.

- Power Plant: $3,000, makes 600 MW, upkeep $50 a month. It is a 2x2 building and needs a clear 2x2 square. It pollutes heavily (55 pollution units, the biggest single source in the game) and puffs smoke from its chimney. It is also the only building that can suffer a nuclear meltdown from the Disasters window.
- Solar Farm: $1,500, makes 150 MW, upkeep $5 a month. One tile, no pollution.
- Wind Farm: $1,000, makes 100 MW on flat ground, upkeep $8 a month. One tile, no pollution. It makes more the higher it stands: each hill tier adds 25%, so it makes 125, 150 or 175 MW on the three higher terraces.

Building on hill ground costs 1.6 times the listed price. A megawatt from solar costs twice what it does from the plant, which is the trade: clean air for more land and money. The inspector on a wind farm says "(hill bonus)" when it is getting extra output, or "(build on hills for more)" when it is not.

## Connecting a generator with power lines
In Vibe City a generator only delivers power into a power line that touches it. A power plant with no line beside any of its four tiles makes nothing the city can use, and the game warns you: "Plant built - run a power line off it to reach the city".

Solar and wind farms daisy-chain. A farm is connected if it touches a line, or if it touches another solar or wind farm that is connected, through any unbroken run of farms. Solar and wind mix freely in a chain, so you can build a whole field of farms and run one line to its edge. The power plant does not join a chain; it always wants its own line.

Double-click a generator with the Pan and Inspect tool to open its tile details. The Grid row says "On a line" or "No power line beside it", and the Supplies row shows its output in MW, or "0 MW - nothing to deliver into".

## Power lines and poles
The Power Line tool costs $8 per tile, $48 per tile over water (six times, carried on concrete piers), and 1.6 times the price on hills. Upkeep is $1 per tile per month. You can drag to lay a run.

A power line goes on grass or water. It can also cross a road or a railway: the road or track stays where it is and the wire runs overhead on poles. Laying a road or rail over an existing line keeps the line crossing overhead too. Poles are placed automatically at ends, corners, junctions, either side of a crossing, and at least every 4 tiles along a straight run.

Wooden poles on land can catch fire. A burnt pole drops its wire, cutting the line there, and everything beyond goes dark until you rebuild that tile for $8. Poles on water piers, and lines crossing a road or railway, do not burn.

## How power reaches buildings
Power in Vibe City spreads in three steps, in the SimCity 2000 style.

1. A generator pushes power onto the line tiles touching it, and power runs along connected line tiles.
2. A live line powers every building within 1 tile of it, including diagonals, so a line down a street feeds the lots on both sides.
3. Any powered building passes power to every power-using building touching one of its four sides, and so on through the whole block. Zoned lots conduct even while they are still empty.

A road between two blocks breaks the chain, because a road does not carry power. Run a line across the road (it crosses overhead) to join the blocks. Inside a built-up block you do not need a wire on every street; lines are for reaching districts.

Things that use power: residential, commercial and industrial lots, water pumps, police, fire stations, schools, hospitals, libraries, universities, the prison, rail depots, the seaport, casinos, clubs, the track and the arcade. Water towers, parks, trees, plazas, statues and the art gallery need no power.

## How much power the city uses
Every connected building draws megawatts:

- Residential: 3 MW per building level. Commercial: 4 MW per level. Industrial: 5 MW per level.
- Water pump: 20 MW.
- Police 8, Fire Station 8, School 10, Hospital 16, University 30, Library 6, Prison 3, Rail Depot 6, Seaport 4, Casino 10, Club 10, Arcade 12, Track 27 (3 per tile of its 3x3 lot).

The City Stats panel, under Power, shows "Plants on a line" (connected generators out of all you own), Output, Draw and Balance in MW. A heatwave multiplies the whole draw by 1.35 and a cold snap by 1.40, so carry a margin. Only generators on a line count toward Output.

## What happens without power
A lot with no power in Vibe City is in trouble:

- An empty zoned lot with no power is built on at one fifth of the normal rate.
- A built lot with no power loses a level with an 8% chance every month, so an unpowered district empties out.
- A civic building with no power is closed ("Closed - no power"). Its coverage, jobs and any service it provides switch off.
- A water pump with no power stops pumping, so losing power can take the water system down with it.

If the city's total draw is higher than total output (a shortage, sometimes called a brownout), every connected building shows the no-power badge, the city's mood counts all of it as unpowered, and the newspaper runs "Power Crisis Grips City". Power is the biggest single factor in the city mood at 24% of the score. Build another generator before the balance goes negative.

## Viewing the grid in the Utilities view
Press V to cycle the map views until you reach Utilities (or use the View button on the top bar). In the Utilities view the grid is drawn in amber: bright where current reaches it, dark where a line is laid but connected to nothing that generates. Generators show as square markers, red when a plant has no line beside it. A red water pump in this view most often means no power reaches it, so the grid is drawn on the same sheet to help you find the break.

In the Utilities view the Bulldoze tool removes only power lines, pipes and tunnels, never the road above them. Each removal costs $5.

## Common questions
**Q: Why is my new power plant not powering anything?**
A: A generator only sends power into a power line touching it. Run a power line from any tile of the plant to the city. The inspector's Grid row will say "On a line" once it is connected.

**Q: Do I need a power line down every street?**
A: No. Powered buildings pass power to every power user touching them, so a whole block lights up from one line. Roads break the chain, so run a line across a road to reach the next block.

**Q: Which generator is cheapest per megawatt?**
A: The power plant: $3,000 for 600 MW. Solar is $1,500 for 150 MW and wind is $1,000 for 100 MW (up to 175 MW on the highest hills), but neither pollutes and their upkeep is $5 and $8 a month against the plant's $50.

**Q: Where should I build wind farms?**
A: On high ground. Each hill tier adds 25% to the 100 MW base, up to 175 MW on the top terrace.

**Q: Do solar farms each need their own power line?**
A: No. Solar and wind farms that touch each other share a connection, so one line to the edge of a field of farms connects them all.

**Q: What does a power shortage do?**
A: When draw exceeds output, buildings show the no-power badge, the city mood treats them as unpowered, and the paper reports a power crisis. Fix it by adding generators.

**Q: Why did part of my city go dark after a fire?**
A: Wooden power poles burn. A burnt pole cuts the line, and everything beyond it loses power until you rebuild that tile of line for $8.

**Q: Can I put a power line across water?**
A: Yes. It costs six times as much, $48 a tile, and stands on concrete piers that do not burn.

**Q: Can a solar or wind farm melt down?**
A: No. The nuclear meltdown disaster needs a power plant. A city running only on solar and wind cannot have one.
