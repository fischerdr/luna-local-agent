# Vibe City: Manual index
Audience: player and developer
Keywords: index, contents, manual, help, guide, table of contents, Vibe City, where to find, topics
Summary: Vibe City is an isometric city builder in the style of SimCity 2000, made by Ryan, that runs in a browser and as a desktop app. This index lists every manual file and what it covers. Files starting with player are for playing the game, files starting with dev are for working on its code.

## About this manual
This manual describes Vibe City release 1.0.0 as of build 547. It is written to be searched in pieces: every file covers one topic, every section names its subject, and every file ends with common questions and answers. When player and developer files disagree in tone, the player files describe what you see in the game and the developer files describe the code that does it.

## Player files
- player-01-getting-started.md: Getting Started. How to start playing Vibe City, from the loading screen and the main menu to generating a new city, saving and loading, and the Settings window. It also covers the three game speeds, how long a month lasts, and what is and is not kept between sessions.
- player-02-controls-and-camera.md: Controls and Camera. How to move around and operate Vibe City with the mouse and keyboard: panning, zooming, rotating, every hotkey, the toolbar and its drawers, and dragging to build with a price preview. It also explains the top bar readouts, the view modes button, tooltips and the tile inspector.
- player-03-zoning-and-growth.md: Zoning and Growth. How residential, commercial and industrial zones work in Vibe City, how lots get built on and climb through four density levels, and what drives demand. It lists everything that speeds growth up, holds it back or makes buildings shrink, so you can work out why a district is or is not growing.
- player-04-transport.md: Transport. How to build and use roads, bridges, railways, rail depots, tunnels and level crossings in Vibe City, what trains do and how to buy a ticket to ride one. It also explains the traffic on the roads, connections to neighbouring towns, and how transport affects growth, pollution and the budget.
- player-05-terrain.md: Terrain, Water and Trees. How the ground works in Vibe City: the five Land tools (Raise, Lower, Level, Water and Tree), what each costs and the rules for using them. It also explains how maps are generated, how height and water affect building costs and placement, and what can be built on hills and shores.
- player-06-power.md: Power Plants, Power Lines and the Grid. In Vibe City power comes from three kinds of generator: the power plant, the solar farm and the wind farm. Power lines carry it to the city, and buildings that have power pass it on to the buildings touching them. Without power, lots barely grow, built lots empty out, and civic buildings and water pumps shut down.
- player-07-water.md: Water Pipes, Pumps, Towers and Drought. In Vibe City water comes only from water pumps on the shore, which need power. Buried pipes carry it to buildings within one tile, and water towers store spare water as a reservoir. Lots without water can still be built but never grow past level 1, and civic buildings close without it.
- player-08-services.md: Civic Services - Police, Fire, Health, Education and Prison. Vibe City has seven civic buildings in the Civic group: police station, fire station, school, hospital, library, university and prison. Each needs both power and water to work, costs monthly upkeep, and gives a growth bonus to lots within its coverage radius. Police also fight crime and chase robbers, fire stations send engines to fires, and a prison is needed once the city passes 3,000 people.
- player-09-money-and-budget.md: Money, Taxes, Budget and Milestones. In Vibe City you start with $10,000, $20,000 or $50,000 depending on difficulty, and earn money each month from a single tax rate on residents and jobs. Upkeep for roads, utilities, civic buildings and decor is paid monthly. Population and age milestones pay cash gifts, and the City Stats panel shows the full budget, tax slider and the advisor.
- player-10-problems-and-disasters.md: Pollution, Crime, Fires and Disasters. Vibe City has two map-wide problems, pollution and crime, each shown on its own data view and each lowering demand where it is bad. Fires, robberies and storms happen at random, while flood, tornado, earthquake, meltdown, alien invasion and firestorm happen only when you choose them in the Disasters window. This page explains each one and how to prevent or handle it.
- player-11-neighbours-news-advisor.md: Neighbouring Towns, the Newspaper, Events and the Advisor. Four towns lie off the edges of the Vibe City map, and a road or railway opened to one of them raises demand at home. The city's own newspaper, the Chronicle, prints a new edition every minute of play with stories about your city. The mayor's advisor in City Stats shows the city mood with one of five faces and names the biggest thing bothering people.
- player-12-parks-and-decor.md: Parks, Plazas, Statues and Trees. Parks, plazas, statues and trees are the cheap decor pieces in Vibe City. They give a small growth boost to lots within 4 tiles, trees also clean the air, and parks and plazas are where a fair can set up. Clicking a park with the pan tool opens a picture with one of the arcade kids talking to you.
- player-13-characters.md: The Characters. Vibe City has a small cast of named characters who talk to you, the Mayor: four arcade kids, six casino dealers, two bookies at the Track, the advisor, the archivist, the teacher and five station ticket clerks. This page lists where each one appears, what they are like, and how the one unlockable character, Rue, is unlocked.
- player-14-arcade.md: The Arcade. The Arcade is a $9,000 one tile venue in Vibe City that boosts growth around it with no crime. Click it with the pan tool to play two cabinets, Neon Trails and Maze Courier, against the arcade kids. Beating the kids at Neon Trails moves them up their ladders, unlocks Rue, and makes every arcade in town reach further.
- player-15-casino.md: The Casino (Blackjack, Poker and Slots). The Casino is a $5,000 nightlife building in Vibe City. Click it with the pan tool to play blackjack, poker (Casino Hold'em) or the slot machine with the city's own money. Every bet comes out of the treasury and every win goes back into it, and big swings make the newspaper.
- player-16-race-track.md: The Race Track and Betting on Horses. The Track is a $10,000 three by three venue in Vibe City that boosts growth within 8 tiles and gives 24 jobs. Click it with the pan tool to bet the city's money on five horse races with a bookie. A winning bet returns the stake times the odds plus the stake.
- player-17-nightlife-and-culture.md: Nightlife and Culture (Club, Gallery, Library, Radio and Sound). This page covers Vibe City's culture and nightlife buildings, the Club with its dancers, the Art Gallery and the Library, plus the Radio music player and the sound settings. The Club is nightlife that brings crime; the Gallery and Library boost growth without it. The Radio plays the soundtrack, and Settings has separate Music, Sound and Interface volumes.
- player-18-seaport-and-ships.md: The Seaport and Ships. The Seaport is a $6,000 two by two dock that goes on the shore in Vibe City. With power, water and a way out to open sea, ships call at it, it lifts industrial demand across the city and gives 20 jobs. A coastal city past 2,500 people needs one, or its industry is held back.
- player-19-desktop-app.md: The Desktop App. Vibe City runs in a web browser and as a desktop app for Linux and Windows. The desktop app saves to Documents/Vibe City/saves, lets you add your own gallery pictures and club dancers in Documents/Vibe City, loads mods from Documents/Vibe City/mods, and can show your city's status on Discord. This page covers installing it, where your files go, and how it differs from the browser.

## Developer files
- dev-01-architecture.md: Codebase architecture. Vibe City is a browser city builder written in plain JavaScript with no bundler, no modules and no framework. Every file in js/ is a classic script loaded in a fixed order from index.html, so every top-level function and variable is a global. This file maps the source tree, the core state in js/state.js, the tile types and the two loops (render frame and monthly sim tick).
- dev-02-rendering.md: Rendering pipeline. Vibe City draws its isometric map onto one 2D canvas, back to front, in two passes: a cached ground layer and a live pass for buildings, vehicles and effects. This file explains the projection, the ground cache and how worldChanged invalidates it, the draw order, the sprite tables in js/sprites.js, rotation and the four view modes.
- dev-03-simulation.md: Simulation model. In Vibe City one simulation tick is one in-game month, run by simTick in js/simulation.js on a setInterval. Each tick recomputes the utility networks and coverage, settles pollution and crime, runs the reservoir, recomputes demand, grows or shrinks lots, applies the budget and rolls events. This file lists the order of a tick, the formulas and constants, and which file owns each system.
- dev-04-save-format.md: Save format and loading. A Vibe City save is one JSON file written by cityJSON in js/save.js, holding every tile array plus blocks for events, neighbours, milestones, footprints, the city name, the arcade and mods. Loading goes through fitSaveToWorld, which sets a save from a smaller map into the current 128x128 world, then rebuilds all derived state from the tiles. On the desktop app saves go to a saves folder chosen by release/saves.js.
- dev-05-art-and-sprites.md: Art and Sprite Pipeline. How building art gets into Vibe City, from a generated 1x4 strip to a sliced, fitted and installed sprite. Covers the templates, the screening and slicing tools, the span and nudge numbers the renderer hangs a sprite by, the facing tables, the variant tables in js/sprites.js, and the img/ folder layout.
- dev-06-build-and-release.md: Build, Versioning and Release. How Vibe City is versioned, cache-busted, shipped and packaged. Covers tools/bump.sh, the generated asset lists from tools/make-playlist.sh, the Electron desktop client built by tools/build-client.js, the player folders in Documents, and tools/prune-assets.sh.
- dev-07-testing.md: Testing with prof Rigs and the Suite. Vibe City is tested by node scripts in prof/, called rigs, that drive the real game in headless Chromium through Playwright and print "N failing". tools/suite.sh runs every rig, checks exit codes as well as output, and takes about 17 minutes. This file covers running the suite, writing a rig, and taking headless screenshots.
- dev-08-mods.md: The Mod System. Vibe City mods are folders in Documents/Vibe City/mods that each add one building with its own sprites and, optionally, a mini game window. The desktop shell lists them and serves their files over vcmod://, and js/mods.js turns each into a real tile type. Neon Brew, a cafe with the Barista Rush game, is the example mod.
- dev-09-adding-content.md: Recipes for Adding Content. Step by step recipes for the content Vibe City is built to grow: park pictures with their lines, a new building variant sprite, sound effects and music, a casino dealer, and character portraits. Each recipe names the files, tables and scripts involved.
- dev-10-conventions.md: House Style and Conventions. The standing rules for working on Vibe City: how code and comments are written, the rules for any text a player reads, the brand and character rules, and the shipping rules (release stays 1.0.0, only the build bumps, and index.html always ships with a bump).

## Common questions
**Q: What is Vibe City?**
A: Vibe City is an isometric city building game in the style of SimCity 2000, written in plain JavaScript by Ryan. It runs in a web browser and as a desktop app for Linux and Windows.

**Q: Which version does this manual describe?**
A: Release 1.0.0, build 547. The release number stays 1.0.0 and only the build number goes up.

**Q: Where do I start if I have never played?**
A: Read player-01-getting-started.md, then player-02-controls-and-camera.md and player-03-zoning-and-growth.md.

**Q: Where do I start if I want to change the code?**
A: Read dev-01-architecture.md, then dev-10-conventions.md and dev-06-build-and-release.md.
